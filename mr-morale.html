Mr. Morale & the Big Steppers

Released May 13, 2022

Length: 01:13:

Mr. Morale & the Big Steppers is the fifth studio album by American rapper Kendrick Lamar. It was released on May 13, 2022, through pgLang, Top Dawg Entertainment, Aftermath Entertainment, and Interscope Records. The album is a double album with eighteen tracks, divided into two parts. It arrived five years after DAMN. and marked the end of his time with Top Dawg Entertainment.

The album is the most personal and openly emotional project of his career. Rather than focusing mainly on his neighborhood or on society at large, Lamar turns the attention onto himself, including his flaws, his mistakes, and the weight of being seen as a leader or savior. The title suggests the contrast between the person behind the image and the public figure who carries expectations.

Therapy is a central theme. Lamar discusses his own journey toward healing, including his work to understand his past, his relationships, and the habits that he inherited from the people around him. He looks at generational trauma, infidelity, fatherhood, and the difficulty of being honest with himself, often presenting these topics with little protection or distance.

The album also addresses wider social issues. "Auntie Diaries" deals with acceptance and the difficulty of understanding family members who are transgender, while "Mother I Sober" explores trauma and the effect it has on relationships. Other songs, such as "Savior" and "Rich Spirit," question celebrity culture, social media, and the way admiration can turn into pressure.

The project features guest appearances from Sampha, Ghostface Killah, Beth Gibbons of Portishead, Kodak Black, Baby Keem, Summer Walker, Taylour Paige, Blxst, and Sam Dew. These artists come from different musical backgrounds, which helps the album shift between styles while keeping a consistent emotional focus.

Production was handled by Sounwave, Duval Timothy, Bēkon, Pharrell Williams, Boi-1da, and many others. The sound is spacious and often stripped back, with piano, soft vocals, and sparse beats that leave room for Lamar's voice. At other moments the album becomes more aggressive and energetic, showing the range of moods it covers.

Lamar's performances change from song to song, as he moves between calm, confessional delivery and sharp, angry verses. On several tracks he adopts different voices and characters, adding to the conversational and sometimes theatrical feel of the album. This variety reflects the way the project deals with many sides of his personality.

Mr. Morale & the Big Steppers debuted at number one on the US Billboard 200 with about 295,000 units in its first week. It received positive reviews from critics, who praised its honesty and ambition, although some listeners found it more challenging than his previous work. The singles "The Heart Part 5" and "N95" helped introduce the project before its release.

The album won Best Rap Album at the Grammy Awards and was supported by the Big Steppers Tour, which turned its themes into a stage show. It is now seen as a turning point in Lamar's career, one that moves away from the role of a spokesperson and toward a more vulnerable and independent artistic voice. Its focus on healing, accountability, and honesty makes it one of his most personal and thought-provoking projects.
