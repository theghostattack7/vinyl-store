good kid, m.A.A.d city

Released October 22, 2012

Length: 01:08:16

good kid, m.A.A.d city is the second studio album by American rapper Kendrick Lamar. It was released on October 22, 2012, through Top Dawg Entertainment, Aftermath Entertainment, and Interscope Records, and was his major-label debut. The album is presented as "a short film by Kendrick Lamar" and tells a story based on his teenage years in Compton, California. It contains twelve tracks on the standard edition, with additional songs on the deluxe version.

The album is a concept project that follows a single day and night in the life of a young Kendrick. Each song moves the story forward, showing how peer pressure, violence, romance, and temptation affect him as he tries to stay out of trouble in his neighborhood. Because the tracks are arranged like scenes in a film, the album is often described as one of the most cinematic projects in modern hip-hop.

The title plays on the phrase "m.A.A.d," which stands for "my angry adolescence divided," and also refers to the contrast between being a good kid and living in a difficult environment. Throughout the album, Lamar looks back at his younger self with a mix of honesty, humor, and regret. The result is a project that is both personal and critical of the world around him.

A recurring feature of the album is the use of skits and voicemails, including messages from Lamar's parents. These moments add humor and warmth while also keeping the story grounded in real family life. The closing songs bring the narrative to a reflective ending, where Lamar considers faith, responsibility, and the idea of using his experiences to help others.

The album includes guest appearances from Drake, MC Eiht, Jay Rock, Anna Wise, and Dr. Dre, with additional collaborators on the deluxe edition. These guests support the story without taking attention away from Lamar's perspective. MC Eiht's appearance on the title track is especially notable because it connects the album to the history of Compton rap.

Production comes from a wide group of producers, including Dr. Dre, Just Blaze, Pharrell Williams, Hit-Boy, Terrace Martin, Sounwave, and T-Minus. The sound moves between dark, atmospheric beats and smoother, more melodic songs. This mix helps the album change mood as the story develops, from tense street scenes to reflective moments of regret and relief.

The album produced several well-known songs, including "Swimming Pools (Drank)," "Bitch, Don't Kill My Vibe," "Poetic Justice," "Backseat Freestyle," and "Money Trees." Each of these tracks shows a different side of Lamar's writing, from critical looks at alcohol and peer pressure to more playful and melodic moments. Their popularity helped bring the album to a much larger audience.

good kid, m.A.A.d city debuted at number two on the US Billboard 200, selling around 242,000 copies in its first week. It received widespread critical acclaim and was nominated for Best Rap Album at the Grammy Awards. The album stayed on the Billboard 200 for years and has since been certified multi-platinum, showing how strongly it continued to connect with listeners.

Today the album is widely regarded as a modern classic and one of the defining releases of the 2010s. It established Lamar as one of the most important storytellers in hip-hop and set the standard for the detailed, narrative-driven albums that followed. Its portrait of Compton, growing up, and finding direction continues to influence artists and fans.
