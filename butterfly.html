To Pimp a Butterfly

Released March 15, 2015

Length: 01:18:51

To Pimp a Butterfly is the third studio album by American rapper Kendrick Lamar. It was released on March 15, 2015, a week earlier than originally planned, through Top Dawg Entertainment, Aftermath Entertainment, and Interscope Records. The album is a large and ambitious project that combines hip-hop with jazz, funk, soul, and spoken word. It was received as a bold step forward after the success of his previous album.

The title carries several layers of meaning. It plays on the novel To Kill a Mockingbird and also references Tupac Shakur, whose name was part of the album's early working title. The butterfly image suggests transformation, while the word "pimp" is used to talk about exploitation and the way success can be used or abused. These ideas give the album a strong symbolic structure.

Thematically, the album deals with race, identity, fame, depression, and self-worth. Lamar reflects on the pressures that came with his sudden rise, the guilt of leaving his community, and the struggle to stay true to himself while the world demands more from him. At the same time, he looks at the history of Black America and the social problems that still shape everyday life.

A poem that runs through the album, revealed line by line over several tracks, ties the project together. It is finally recited in full on the closing song, "Mortal Man," where Lamar holds an imagined conversation with Tupac using archival interview audio. This ending turns the album into a personal and philosophical statement about leadership, legacy, and responsibility.

The album features a large group of collaborators, including Thundercat, Flying Lotus, Terrace Martin, Kamasi Washington, Robert Glasper, George Clinton, Bilal, Snoop Dogg, Rapsody, Anna Wise, and Pharrell Williams. Many of these musicians come from the jazz and funk worlds, which gave the project a live, organic feel and made it different from most mainstream rap albums of the time.

Production was led by Sounwave, Terrace Martin, Flying Lotus, and Thundercat, among others. Live bass lines, horns, and layered instrumentation replace many of the standard rap production techniques. This allows songs to shift in tempo and mood, and it supports Lamar's experimental vocal style, which ranges from smooth rapping to shouted and distorted performances.

Songs such as "King Kunta," "i," "The Blacker the Berry," and "These Walls" became widely discussed highlights. "Alright" had an especially large cultural impact. Its chorus became a rallying cry at protests and public gatherings, and the song was embraced as an anthem of hope during a period of social tension in the United States.

To Pimp a Butterfly debuted at number one on the US Billboard 200 with around 324,000 units in its first week. Critics praised it enthusiastically, and many ranked it among the best albums of the decade. At the Grammy Awards it received eleven nominations and won Best Rap Album, along with several other awards for its songs and related work.

The album is now considered one of the most important hip-hop albums of its generation. Its mix of jazz, politics, personal reflection, and musical risk-taking expanded what a rap album could be, and it has influenced artists across many genres. For many listeners, it remains the clearest example of Lamar's ability to combine artistic ambition with a message that speaks to a wide audience.
