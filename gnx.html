GNX

Released November 22, 2024

Length: 44:20

GNX is the sixth studio album by American rapper Kendrick Lamar. It was surprise-released on November 22, 2024, through PGLang and Interscope Records. Unlike a traditional album rollout, Lamar announced the project with very little advance promotion, allowing the music to reach listeners unexpectedly. The album contains twelve tracks and has a runtime of just under forty-five minutes, making it a relatively compact project while still covering a wide range of musical ideas and performances.

The title GNX refers to the Buick Regal GNX, a high-performance American car produced in the 1980s. The reference connects the album to ideas of American car culture, speed, power, and West Coast identity. The GNX also has a strong connection to California culture, which fits with the album's repeated references to Los Angeles, Compton, and the wider West Coast. Throughout the project, Lamar uses these references to establish a sense of place and to connect his current work with the musical traditions that influenced him.

GNX represents an important transition in Lamar's career. The album was released following his departure from Top Dawg Entertainment and Aftermath Entertainment, the labels associated with many of the defining releases of his earlier career. With PGLang playing a central role in the project, GNX presents a more independent chapter in Lamar's artistic development. This change allowed him to continue developing his own creative identity while working with a wide range of producers, musicians, and artists.

The album features guest appearances from SZA, Dody6, Lefty Gunplay, Wallie the Sensei, Siete7x, Roddy Ricch, AzChike, Hitta J3, YoungThreat, and Peysoh. These collaborations give the album a strong connection to contemporary West Coast rap. Rather than relying exclusively on established mainstream artists, Lamar also works with several younger and regional performers, giving different voices a place within the project. The combination of established artists and newer performers contributes to the album's diverse sound and its strong connection to the California hip-hop scene.

Production on GNX was primarily handled by Kendrick Lamar, Sounwave, and Jack Antonoff, with additional contributions from Mustard, Sean Momberger, Kamasi Washington, and other musicians. The production combines heavy basslines, sharp percussion, atmospheric elements, vocal samples, and layered instrumentation. Many of the tracks have an energetic and aggressive sound, while others create more space for Lamar's vocals and storytelling. This contrast allows the album to move between different moods without losing its overall identity.

One of the most noticeable characteristics of GNX is its connection to West Coast hip-hop. Lamar incorporates elements associated with California rap while combining them with modern production techniques. The album draws from the history of the region without simply reproducing older styles. This creates a sound that can feel nostalgic while remaining contemporary. References to Los Angeles, local culture, cars, neighborhoods, and regional artists appear throughout the project and help create a consistent sense of location.

Lamar's performances also change throughout the album. On some tracks, he uses an aggressive delivery with rapid changes in rhythm and vocal tone, while other songs rely more heavily on melody and layered vocals. This variation gives the project a dynamic structure and allows Lamar to move between different styles of rap. His delivery is often closely connected to the production, with changes in his voice and rhythm helping emphasize the energy of individual tracks.

GNX was supported by four singles: "Squabble Up", "Luther", "TV Off", and "Peekaboo". "Squabble Up" is driven by energetic production and a strong rhythmic structure, while "Luther" takes a more melodic approach through its collaboration with SZA. "TV Off" features Mustard and includes one of the album's most recognizable high-energy performances. "Peekaboo", featuring AzChike, continues the album's emphasis on West Coast rap and regional collaboration.

The album also received significant attention for its relationship to Lamar's earlier work and his broader artistic identity. GNX continues several themes that have appeared throughout his discography, including identity, fame, competition, community, family, and his relationship with his hometown. At the same time, the album often presents a more energetic and celebratory atmosphere than some of his previous releases. This combination of personal themes and high-energy performances gives GNX a different character while maintaining elements of Lamar's established style.

GNX received widespread critical acclaim and became Lamar's fifth number-one album on the US Billboard 200. It also reached number one in several other countries, including Canada, the Netherlands, Sweden, Australia, Denmark, and the United Kingdom. The album's surprise release generated considerable attention online and helped create a strong initial response from listeners. Its commercial performance demonstrated the continued global reach of Lamar's music and the ability of a relatively short promotional campaign to generate substantial interest.

The album's success also extended beyond streaming and album sales. Lamar and SZA later performed together during the Super Bowl LIX halftime show, bringing several of their collaborations to one of the largest television audiences in the United States. The performance further connected GNX with Lamar's broader public profile and followed a period of significant attention surrounding his music and live performances.

In 2025, Lamar and SZA embarked on the Grand National Tour in support of GNX and SZA's Lana. The tour brought the two artists together for a large series of performances across multiple locations. The concerts allowed Lamar to translate the album's energetic production and visual identity into a live setting, while also incorporating material from different stages of his career.

GNX also received substantial recognition from the Recording Academy. The album and its songs received eight nominations at the 68th Grammy Awards and won four awards. GNX won Best Rap Album, while "Luther" won Record of the Year and Best Melodic Rap Performance. "TV Off" won Best Rap Song. These awards added to Lamar's extensive history of recognition at the Grammys and highlighted the impact of the album's music and collaborations.

Beyond its commercial and award recognition, GNX can be viewed as another stage in Kendrick Lamar's development as an artist. The project combines references to his roots with newer sounds, collaborators, and production approaches. Its emphasis on West Coast culture gives the album a strong regional identity, while its collaborations and modern production allow it to reach listeners outside that tradition. The result is a project that connects different generations and styles of hip-hop while remaining centered around Lamar's distinctive voice and perspective.

With its surprise release, strong regional identity, varied production, and extensive collaborations, GNX occupies a distinctive place within Kendrick Lamar's discography. The album demonstrates his continued interest in experimenting with different sounds while maintaining a connection to the culture and communities that shaped his music. From its title and artwork to its production, collaborations, and live performances, GNX presents a project strongly connected to the history and contemporary identity of West Coast hip-hop.
